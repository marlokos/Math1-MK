Teacher guide

Suggested flow

  1. Project the page. Before pressing Start, ask: "Which cup will be fuller after 30 seconds? Will they ever be equal?"
  2. Run the pour live with Exact readings. Students jot a few readings as they print.
  3. Groups find each cup's rate and starting amount, write equations and test them with Model it. Then answer questions 1–4.
  4. Switch to Real sensor and press Pour again. The points no longer fit a perfect line, so groups fit a line by eye.
  5. Reveal the regression lines. Compare slopes, intercepts and r for the two cups, and with the exact run.
  6. Move on to Part B, where the data is much messier.

Discussion questions

  • Where does the rate show up in the table? On the graph? In the equation?
  • Why does the starting amount mean ice, not soda?
  • Should the graph be a line or separate points? What happens between readings?
  • With real sensor data, why is r close to 1 but not equal to 1?
  • Would you trust the model to predict 3 minutes? What happens at the rim?

Standards: A-CED.2, F-IF.4, F-LE.5, A-REI.3, A-REI.6, A-REI.11, S-ID.6, S-ID.7, S-ID.8.

    2. Which value is closest to the correlation coefficient r?

    Teacher guide

    Suggested flow

    1. Before starting, ask: "Who do you think visits a fast-food place more often, younger or older customers? How sure are you?"
    2. Start the week. Pause around Wednesday and ask students to describe the pattern so far, then predict Sunday's.
    3. When the week ends, groups try at least three lines in the lab and pick the one they think fits best.
    4. Add the regression line and compare it with each group's best line. Use Show residuals to talk about how far individual members are from the line.
    5. Answer questions 1–5. Then load a new week and compare: is the pattern the same?

    Discussion questions

    • How did the scatter plot change from Monday to Sunday? When could you first see the trend?
    • Why is it hard to agree on one line of best fit when the correlation is weak? What does r tell us about that?
    • What does the y-intercept mean here? Does a 0-year-old visit 5 times a week?
    • Does getting older cause fewer visits? What else could explain the pattern?
    • Would the model work for a 95-year-old? What about a 5-year-old?
